## Proposal text (final)
Each of you pastes this into your own TrueNorthLogic form. Two bracketed sentences differ per teacher; everything else is identical. Fill the [observation] sentence truthfully from your own feedback; if you have none yet, use the conversation with the evaluator alone.
Domain: 1 — Classroom Strategies and Behaviors
Design Question: 1 — Communicating Learning Goals and Feedback
What do I want to see improve in my practice as a result of focusing on this targeted strategy?
I want to track student progress on each MA.7 benchmark the week it is taught, instead of waiting for the unit test, and use that data within two school days to decide what to reteach. [In my most recent observation feedback on Element 1, learning goals and performance scales, I was asked to show how students track their own progress against the scale, and I discussed this focus with (evaluator name) before writing this plan.] My 7th grade math teammate, [John Shaffer / Croix Shaffer], teaches the same courses, so we will give a common weekly check in one on-level section each and make reteach decisions together. Our on-level 7th grade proficiency is the school's lowest data point, and I want a faster loop between what I taught and what students actually learned.
What is my expected outcome?
Students will see each week's benchmark posted as a learning goal with a four-level scale, know how they did, and record it on a progress strip they keep. Our team will have a shared record of mastery by benchmark for both sections, with a reteach decision logged for every check. Mastery means 4 or more correct out of 5 on a weekly check. I expect re-check results on retaught benchmarks to exceed the first check in at least four of every five reteach cycles, by more than the change on comparison items from a benchmark that was not retaught, and I expect the gap between each retaught benchmark and the class average to narrow on the unit assessment in Focus.
Action Steps
- 
Complete at least one district or school professional learning session on formative assessment, performance scales, or data-driven reteaching by the end of Q2, apply one strategy from it to how I write the weekly scale or run the reteach lesson, and name the session and strategy in my Q2 reflection.
- 
For each weekly check, post a four-level performance scale for the benchmark in student language (level 2: the foundational skill; level 3: the benchmark; level 4: application), print it on the check, and have students mark their level and record their score on a progress strip kept in their notebook.
- 
Give a common 5-item check on the week's MA.7 benchmark in one on-level section, the same check my teammate gives, in each full instructional week beginning the week of October 26; unit-test weeks, weeks before a break, and short weeks are logged as "no check." [Croix: Items come from a benchmark-tagged item bank I maintain, with the key labeled by benchmark and depth of knowledge. / John: Before each check is printed, I review the five items with my teammate for alignment to the benchmark and rigor.]
- 
Score each check within the week, record every student's score and level on a class tally sheet, note the item that gave the most trouble, and keep all tally sheets in a dated binder as my record, with results entered by student code in a shared tracking sheet in our school Google Drive.
- 
Within two school days of each check, hold a brief data huddle with my teammate, in person or on paper, to identify the lowest-mastery item and decide what will be retaught, and log the date, benchmark, and decision. The following week, reteach that benchmark in a short targeted lesson and give a 5-item re-check as practice: three items on the retaught benchmark and two on a recent benchmark that was not retaught, so the gain can be compared. If my teammate cannot give the check in a given week, I will still give it, record my results, and log the decision.
- 
Save a snapshot of the tracking sheet with a written reflection at the end of Q2, Q3, and April, and keep three anonymized student progress strips per quarter as artifacts.
How will I determine positive impact?
- 
Reteach cycle results from the tracking sheet: paired first-check vs. re-check percent correct on each retaught benchmark, alongside the change on the comparison items that were not retaught, pooled across cycles.
- 
Unit assessment results in Focus: each retaught benchmark's gap below the class average on the first check compared with its gap on the unit assessment.
- 
The huddle log (date, benchmark, item, decision), showing the team's decisions and what changed as a result.
- 
Completion artifacts with captions: sample checks with the printed scale, answer keys, a blank tally sheet, a returned check with the level marked (student code only), and student progress strips.
## The plan on one page
One on-level period each. One benchmark a week. A 5-item check on Thursday that students score themselves, a reteach on Tuesday, a 5-item re-check on Wednesday that carries its own control group. Everything on paper lives in one binder in John's room; everything digital lives in one school-account Google Sheet that only Croix touches.
Why it holds up:
- 
Every action step in the submitted text can be completed as written, in a bad week, by one teacher alone.
- 
The evidence is a single table the sheet builds itself: one row per reteach cycle, with the comparison change beside the gain. It answers regression to the mean, practice effects, small n, and selection in one page.
- 
John's part is a binder, a key read aloud, and a half-sheet. He also holds his own artifact.
- 
Net new instructional time is close to zero because the check, reteach, and re-check replace existing bellwork and exit-ticket slots.
- 
It is a DQ1 / Element 1 plan on the observation side (posted scale, student progress strips) and a team-collaboration plan on the DPP side (huddle log), which are the two things Lake County actually scores.
Hard constraints respected: John opens no software. Nothing on GitHub Pages or Apps Script. Croix at ~25 minutes a week, honestly counted, with the automation that gets it to ~20 built after the loop has survived a practice week. Accelerated sections are out of the DPP.

Minimum viable week: check given in both rooms, tally sheets in the binder, decision line filled. That counts as a cycle. Reteach and re-check are expected but a week that gets only the minimum is logged, not lost.

## Scope, grading rule, and the real calendar
Scope: one matched on-level period each, chosen now. Pick the two sections that meet closest to the same time of day with the most similar makeup. Croix may still give the check in his other on-level sections (students self-score and keep the strip; nothing is entered), which keeps the scale and strip visible in every room for walkthroughs without adding entry. Accelerated is out of the DPP: its pacing can't absorb 25 minutes a week and it doubled every step.
Grading rule, identical in both rooms: 5 classwork points for completing the check and filling in the strip, never for the score. Keeps it in the 25% classwork bucket, away from the 70% assessment bucket and the retake policy, and gives 7th graders a reason to try. John already enters classwork grades in Focus; this is not new software for him. State it once at the practice week and don't revisit.
The calendar as it is. Q2–Q4 is about 30 calendar weeks. Remove Thanksgiving week, the week before winter break, 3-day weeks, spring break, FAST PM2 and PM3 windows, roughly six unit-test weeks, and the last two weeks of May, and there are about 16–18 checkable weeks. ## Paper kit and the binder
One binder, in John's room, never leaves. It is the paper half of the evidence for both teachers.
Binder contents
- 
Front pocket: the one-page calendar of check weeks, the backstop check for the quarter (class sets + key), and the paper name key (student code → name) for both sections. The name key exists only here.
- 
One tab per week, in order: both teachers' tally sheets, Croix's photographed and entered, plus a copy of that week's check and key.
- 
Back pocket: blank tally sheets and blank progress strips.
The weekly check (half-sheet, two per page)
- 
Header: check ID (W12), benchmark code, the four-level scale in student language, calculator rule.
- 
Five items, one benchmark: 2 fluency, 2 application, 1 that needs the reasoning the benchmark names. Space to work.
- 
Footer: "Score __/5  My level: 1 2 3 4" and a student-code box. No names on the check itself.

- 
Check ID and teacher at the top; two columns of student codes with a score box and a level box each. Absent = A.
- 
Summary boxes: count of 5s, 4s, 3-and-under.
- 
"Item that gave the most trouble: ___" (teacher fills Thursday).
- 
"Reteach decision: ___  Date: ___  In person / Paper" (Croix fills Monday). This line is the huddle record.
- 
Re-check row added Wednesday: "/3 retaught  /2 comparison" per student.
The progress strip (quarter-page, one per student per quarter)
- 
Rows for each week: check ID, score /5, level circled, re-check /3 and /2.
- 
Lives in the student's notebook. This is the Example 3 artifact and the thing a walkthrough can see. Three per quarter, student code only, go in the DPP folder.

Setup, once (Croix, 30 min): print the calendar, assign codes from both printed Focus rosters (John hands you his; no export needed), print a quarter of blank tally sheets and strips, put the first backstop check in the pocket.

CYCLES computes, per reteach cycle (one row each):
- 
Benchmark; n paired (students with both a first-check and a re-check score).
- 
First-check % correct on the benchmark (paired students only) → re-check % correct on the 3 retaught items → gain.
- 
Comparison benchmark's % correct on its own first check → % on the 2 comparison items → comparison change.
- 
Net gain = gain − comparison change. This is the number that survives "wouldn't they have improved anyway?"
- 
Unit-test gap: the benchmark's gap below class average at first check vs on the unit test (from UNITS).
- 
Summary row: pooled means, count of cycles with net gain > 0, total n.
Also on CYCLES, kept off the submitted evidence: the two sections side by side per check. Useful at the huddle for catching a misprinted item or a pacing miss; not informative as impact.
The one exhibit for the final meeting: the CYCLES table printed, with a dumbbell chart beside it: one row per cycle, a line from first-check % to re-check % on the retaught benchmark, and a grey tick showing the comparison change on the same row. One caption sentence: "Each retaught benchmark is paired with items from a benchmark that was taught but not retaught; the gap between the colored line and the grey tick is the effect of the reteach." That single page answers regression to the mean, practice effects, difficulty drift, small n, and selection. Nothing else in the package has to.
Privacy: codes only in the sheet; CYCLES shows aggregates; crop account headers from every screenshot. Student-level rows are shown live to an evaluator who asks, never pasted. Download a copy of the sheet to the DPP folder at each quarter end.
## The check bank (built, frozen at edition 4.1)
The checks, keys, re-checks and comparison items for all 34 on-level MA.7 benchmarks are built and frozen. They come from Cadence's generators, run in Math Nation unit order (Units 2–13), and are in the DPP_2026-27 folder, one PDF per benchmark under 01 Weekly checks by unit.
Each benchmark's PDF is six pages:
- 
Pages 1–2, weekly check C-<benchmark>: one sheet, front and back. The learning goal and four-level scale are on the front, five items (four regular, one rigor) follow, and the score line is on the back. Every write-in item has at least 0.6 in of writing room.
- 
Page 3, answer key: worked solutions, plus the scoring rule for select-all and matching items.
- 
Page 4, reteach plan for Tuesday.
- 
Page 5, re-check (-R, 3 items).
- 
Page 6, comparison (-X, 2 items), used on a later week's re-check when this benchmark was not retaught.
Re-check and comparison pages hold two copies when they fit with full-size figures and room to write. Otherwise they hold one, which is the case for 30 of the 68.
Weekly work with the bank: open the unit folder and print pages 1–2 two-sided for the students and page 3 once for the binder. On Monday, print page 5 of the retaught benchmark and page 6 of the comparison benchmark.
Audited four times. Each round, three reviewers read every item and recomputed every key. Edition 4.1 fixed the last high-severity items and the one key error. It also redrew nine re-check and comparison items that reused a check item's numbers: a student who remembered the check would have had a head start, which would inflate the reteach gain the evidence rests on. No re-check or comparison item now reuses a check item's givens or answers.
Frozen. Each Cadence revision used to redraw all 340 items, so every round of fixes drew new problems. The bank is now saved item by item, and a fix changes only its own slot. Cadence work continues separately for next year's edition, through the generator handoff in the folder.

## Risks that remain, and the evaluator's questions
What v2 did not fully solve, and the answers to have ready.
Residual risks
- 
Peer scoring on 5 short-answer items. Kids mis-score. The spot-check of five papers catches drift; if a class is unreliable, the teacher scores that section and it costs 10 minutes. Not a data problem at the pooled level.
- 
The comparison items are from a different week. The control benchmark was taught earlier and sat longer, so its change is a conservative comparison (it will tend to decay, which makes net gain look bigger). Say this out loud at the final meeting rather than letting an evaluator find it; the honest version still beats no control.
- 
Croix is still the single point of failure for printing and entry. The backstop check covers a missed print. Entry can lag two weeks with no harm as long as the binder is complete. If Croix is out a full week, that week is a minimum viable week by rule.
- 
John's evaluator may still want to see "his" system. He shows the binder, a check with the scale, and a student's strip. He explains the huddle line. That is a full DQ1 artifact set that happens to be paper.
- 
February. The calendar and the 14-cycle target are the defense. If cycles are on pace in January, say so in the binder; if not, the plan allows modification (manual, footnote ***) and the Q3 reflection explains why.
- 
The scale. Writing a real 4-level scale per benchmark is the one piece of new thinking each week. Once banked in Cadence it is zero work next year; this year it is 5 minutes of Croix's Monday block and gets better after the PD session.
