BEFORE A: Part A: 0.215. Part B: about 430 times. Part C: about 2 purple marbles (1 or 3 also accepted). Score: 1 point for all three parts; Part A alone earns 0.
AFTER Q: A bag holds 10 marbles. Each marble is green, purple or blue, but the number of each color is not known. A student draws a marble at random, records its color and puts it back. The table shows the results of 200 draws.

Green: 121 | Purple: 43 | Blue: 36

Part A. What is the experimental probability of drawing a purple marble? Write it as a decimal.
Part B. If the student makes 2,000 more draws, about how many times should a purple marble be drawn?
Part C. About how many of the 10 marbles in the bag are purple?
AFTER A: Part A: 0.215. Part B: about 430 times. Part C: about 2 purple marbles. Score: 1 point for all three parts; Part A alone earns 0.
AFTER S: Part A. Experimental probability = purple draws ÷ all draws = 43/200 = 0.215.
Part B. The bag has not changed, so about the same part of the new draws should be purple: 0.215 × 2,000 = 430 times. (The actual count will vary a little.)
Part C. The experimental probability estimates the theoretical probability, which is the number of purple marbles out of 10: 0.215 × 10 = 2.15, so about 2 of the marbles are purple (2/10 = 0.2, close to 0.215).
