## Patterns worth addressing across the bank

- **Referents:** Words such as “holds,” “the population,” and “the survey” should explicitly refer to the *quantity of interest*. Identical containers still differ in how much they **contain**.
- **Students should not have to guess the survey question:** A pie chart labeled with answer categories is clearer when the question being asked is in the stem.
- **Real-world objects should match their sizes:** Large-diameter paper cups, elaborate physical spinners, and unusual price arrangements add avoidable distractions. Preserve numbers; choose better objects.
- **Financial language needs one plain-English explanation:** “Negative balance” makes sense only after clarifying that the student is recording an unpaid amount as negative.
- **Prefer “would expect” to “should happen” in simulated random events:** Students may read “should” as a guaranteed outcome.
- **Retain the existing form-entry reminders:** The real image-based kit already supplies coefficient-only π instructions and generic number-entry instructions. There is no need to append repetitive entry text to each math stem.
