## Patterns across the bank

- **Auto-scoring and notation (highest priority):** Eighteen typed items request answers containing π, but the allowed input alphabet excludes π. Ten typed percentage questions likewise invite students to enter a `%` that the form forbids. Use a consistent, visible coefficient-only / percent-number-only instruction and test the actual answer keys.
- **Exact-value inference from box plots:** Three comparison items imply that a box plot determines which population is *more likely* to contain a particular exact observation. Quartile summaries alone cannot establish that. The revisions assess where the value falls within the quartiles instead.
- **Story realism:** An otherwise routine scale question creates a 7.5-meter doorway; a 24-inch pizza is eaten mostly by one student; a huge oatmeal package is assumed; and some local public-transport categories seem improbable. These can all be fixed without changing computation.
- **Student-facing wording:** A few useful refinements remove unnecessarily loaded storm scenarios, specify what a negative balance represents, define continuous-rate questions at whole-hour observations, and distinguish library/transport survey categories.
- **Assessments as comparable measures:** Revisit whether the 5-item checks, 3-item re-checks, and 2-item controls measure similar content and cognitive demand before interpreting score gains. For instance, counting die faces is not equivalent in demand to analyzing five candidate sample spaces. A clean story alone does not guarantee comparable instruments.
- **Answer equivalence and visuals:** Test exact-match handling for `34`/`34.00`, `−2.4`/`−2.40`, and equivalent fraction or mixed-number strings; verify graph scales, labels, and illustrations against the printed figure descriptions before release.

2. For each typed percent, ensure the key is a numeric percent (for example, `60`, not `0.60` and not `60%`) and the instructions say to omit the `%` sign.
