  L3: I can list or count every outcome in the sample space, with no repeats or misses.
  L2: I can list some of the outcomes of a simple experiment.

## CHECK (5 items, given the week taught) — calculator: yes

### Q1  type: Multiple choice  (generator label: Sample space of a simple experiment)
STEM: Each letter of the word DIFFERENCE is written on its own card, one card for each letter, and the cards are placed in a jar. One card is drawn at random and its letter is recorded. / How many outcomes are in the sample space for this experiment?
  A. 8
  B. 7
  C. 10
  D. 6
KEY: B
SOLUTION: DIFFERENCE has 10 letters, so there are 10 cards, but F is on 2 cards and E is on 3 cards. / The outcome is the letter that is recorded, and a letter that is on more than one card is still one outcome. / The sample space is {D, I, F, E, R, N, C}, which has 7 outcomes, not 10.

### Q2  type: Checkboxes  (generator label: Sample space of a simple experiment)
STEM: A set of cards shows these colored shapes, one on each card: red circle, yellow star, yellow triangle, yellow triangle, yellow star, yellow triangle. One card is drawn at random, and the colored shape on it is recorded. / Select all the outcomes in the sample space for this experiment.
  A. red circle
  B. red star
  C. red triangle
  D. yellow circle
  E. yellow star
  F. yellow triangle
KEY: red circle, yellow star, yellow triangle
SOLUTION: Each colored shape that can be drawn is one outcome: red circle, yellow star and yellow triangle. A shape that is on more than one card is still only one outcome, so the sample space is {red circle, yellow star, yellow triangle}. / Red star, red triangle and yellow circle are not on any card, so they cannot be drawn.

  B. The sample space for the spinner has 5 outcomes, and the sample space for the bag has 6 outcomes.
  C. The two experiments have the same sample space, {orange, green, red, purple}.
  D. The sample space for the bag has one outcome that the sample space for the spinner does not have.
KEY: A
SOLUTION: Each outcome is a color, and a color is listed once no matter how many sections or marbles have it. / Spinner: {orange, green, red, purple}. Bag: {orange, green, red}. / The spinner can land on purple, and the bag has no purple marble, so the sample space for the spinner has one more outcome.

SCALE goal: I can find and interpret the probability of a simple event.
  L4: I can compare probabilities of events from different experiments and explain which is more likely.
  L3: I can write the probability of a simple event as a fraction, decimal, or percent and say what it means.
  L2: I can tell whether an event is likely, unlikely, certain, or impossible.

## CHECK (5 items, given the week taught) — calculator: yes

### Q1  type: Multiple choice  (generator label: Interpret and compare probabilities)
STEM: The probability of rain tomorrow is reported as 75%. / Is this event likely or unlikely? Explain using the probability.
  A. Likely, because 75% is greater than 50% and closer to 1 than to 0.
  B. Unlikely, because 75% is less than 100%, so the event might not happen.
  C. Certain, because 75% is so close to 100% that it must happen.
  D. As likely as not, because 75% is a percentage and not a fraction.
KEY: A
SOLUTION: 75% = 0.75, which is greater than 0.5, so it is closer to 1 than to 0. / An event with a probability less than 0.5 is unlikely and an event with a probability greater than 0.5 is likely, so this event is likely.

### Q2  type: Multiple choice  (generator label: Interpret and compare probabilities)
STEM: Which value cannot be the probability of an event?
  A. 0
  B. 110%
  C. 7/8
  D. 1
KEY: B
SOLUTION: Every probability is between 0 and 1, inclusive (or between 0% and 100%). / 110% cannot be a probability because a probability written as a percent can never be greater than 100%.

### Q3  type: Multiple choice  (generator label: Interpret and compare probabilities)
STEM: The probability that Mia makes her next free throw is 5/8. The probability that Jon makes his next free throw is 1/4. / Which event is more likely?
  A. There is not enough information.
  B. Mia makes her next free throw
  C. Jon makes his next free throw
  D. They are equally likely.
KEY: B
SOLUTION: 5/8 ≈ 0.63 and 1/4 = 0.25. / The greater probability is 5/8, so the event "Mia makes her next free throw" is more likely.

### Q4  type: Multiple choice  (generator label: Interpret and compare probabilities)

Event 2: The probability that a spinner with two equal sections, one red and one blue, lands on red is 1/2.
Event 3: The probability that a hurricane passes within 100 miles of the city in the next two days is 0.6. / Which choice describes Events 1, 2 and 3, in that order?
  A. unlikely, neither likely nor unlikely, likely
  B. likely, neither likely nor unlikely, unlikely
  C. unlikely, neither likely nor unlikely, unlikely
  D. unlikely, likely, likely
KEY: A
SOLUTION: 2% is close to 0, so Event 1 is unlikely. / 1/2 is exactly halfway between 0 and 1, so Event 2 is neither likely nor unlikely. / 0.6 is greater than 1/2, so Event 3 is likely.

### Q5  [flagged as the rigor item]  type: Multiple choice  (generator label: Interpret and compare probabilities)
STEM: A fair 10-sided die has faces numbered 1 through 10. The probability that it rains tomorrow is 0.2. / Which is more likely: that the die shows a number greater than 3, or that it rains tomorrow? Choose the answer that compares the two probabilities correctly.
  A. Rain: 0.2 = 20%, and 20% is more than 7/10 because 20 is greater than 7.
  B. The die: P(greater than 3) = 7/10 = 0.7, which is 0.5 more than 0.2.
  C. The die: P(greater than 3) = 8/10 = 0.8, which is 0.6 more than 0.2.

KEY: B
SOLUTION: The numbers greater than 3 are 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 and 10: 7 of the 10 equally likely faces, so P(greater than 3) = 7/10 = 0.7. / In the same form, 0.7 > 0.2, so the die showing a number greater than 3 is more likely, by 0.7 − 0.2 = 0.5. / 3/10 counts 1, 2 and 3 instead; 8/10 also counts the 3; 20% cannot be compared with 7/10 by comparing 20 with 7.

### Q2  type: Short answer  (generator label: Find theoretical probability of an event)
STEM: A fair spinner has 25 equal sections. 6 sections are green. The spinner is spun once. / What is the probability that the spinner lands on green? Write your answer as a decimal.
KEY: typed: 0.24 | .24
SOLUTION: All 25 sections are equally likely, so the sample space has 25 outcomes and 6 of them are green. / P(green) = 6/25. / 6 ÷ 25 = 0.24.

### Q3  type: Multiple choice  (generator label: Find theoretical probability of an event)

  A. 3/11

  C. 3/4

### Q5  [flagged as the rigor item]  type: Short answer  (generator label: Find theoretical probability of an event)

## CHECK (5 items, given the week taught) — calculator: yes

### Q1  type: Multiple choice  (generator label: Experimental vs theoretical probability)
STEM: A fair 12-sided die is rolled 300 times. It lands on a 9 exactly 24 times. / Find the experimental probability of rolling a 9 and the theoretical probability of rolling a 9, each as a fraction in simplest form. Then compare them. / Which choice is correct?
  A. experimental 2/23, theoretical 1/12; the experimental probability is greater
  B. experimental 2/25, theoretical 1/12; the experimental probability is greater
  C. experimental 2/25, theoretical 1/12; the experimental probability is less
  D. experimental 1/12, theoretical 2/25; the experimental probability is greater
KEY: C
SOLUTION: Experimental: 24 of the 300 rolls landed on 9, so 24/300 = 2/25. / Theoretical: 1 of the 12 equally likely faces is a 9, so 1/12. / 2/25 = 0.08 and 1/12 ≈ 0.083, so the experimental probability is slightly less than the theoretical probability. / 2/23 divides by the 276 rolls that were not a 9 instead of by all 300 rolls.

### Q2  type: Short answer  (generator label: Experimental vs theoretical probability)
STEM: A fair coin is tossed 400 times and lands on heads 204 times. / What is the experimental probability of landing on heads? Write your answer as a decimal.
KEY: typed: 0.51 | .51
SOLUTION: Experimental probability = successes ÷ trials. / 204 ÷ 400 = 0.51.

### Q3  type: Multiple choice  (generator label: Experimental vs theoretical probability)
STEM: Two students each simulate rolling a fair die. One runs 5,900 trials and the other runs 370 trials. / Whose experimental probability is expected to be closer to the theoretical probability?
  A. The 5,900-trial student, because more trials usually bring the result closer to the theory.
  B. Both students equally, because a fair experiment gives the same result for any number of trials.
  C. The 370-trial student, because fewer trials leave fewer chances for a result to go wrong.
  D. Neither student, because an experimental result never comes close to the theoretical one.
KEY: A
